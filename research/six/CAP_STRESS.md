# n=6 containment-support stress

## Motivation

The angle-only quadratic dual in `ANGLE_DUAL.md` already removes all center
coordinates, but its completed-square penalty is not the sharpest way to use
circle containment.

There is a simpler support-function inequality.  It is exact for a single
square in a disk, and at the candidate contact graph it admits a self-stress
with equality.  This is a promising route to a short analytic proof and also
a much faster certificate checker.

## Stress inequality

For a fixed separating-axis graph, orient every selected separator as

[
n_ecdot(p_{j(e)}-p_{i(e)})ge H_e,
]

where (H_e) is the sum of the two square half-widths in direction (n_e).
Choose arbitrary multipliers (mu_ege0), and put

[
G_i=
sum_{e:j(e)=i}mu_e n_e-
sum_{e:i(e)=i}mu_e n_e.
]

Summing the separator inequalities gives

[

Apply (2) to every square center:

[

]

There are no center coordinates in (4).  Unlike the completed-square dual,
(4) uses the exact directional support of the center-feasible set.

To prove (Rge R_*), it is enough to find, for every remaining contact
graph and angle domain, nonnegative multipliers for which the reverse strict
inequality holds when (R<R_*).

For the candidate contact graph use the eight separator inequalities

[

[

With the exact, unrounded stress, (4) is an equality at the known candidate.

The force directions obtained from (5) are not accidental: for (E,N,W,S)
they point toward the corresponding active far vertex of the enclosing
circle.  The (D)-force lies in the normal cone generated by its two active
far vertices.  Thus (5) is the KKT/self-stress of the candidate containment
contacts.

This explains why this dual is substantially sharper than the earlier
generic completed-square relaxation.

## Reflection pair

Reflection in the line (x=y) swaps

[
Eleftrightarrow N,qquad Wleftrightarrow S
]

and exchanges the stress coefficients

[
mu_{CN}leftrightarrowmu_{CE},quad
mu_{WC}leftrightarrowmu_{SC},quad
mu_{WN}leftrightarrowmu_{SE},quad
mu_{DW}leftrightarrowmu_{DS}.
]

The original stress is strongest on one side of the diagonal-square angle,
and its reflected stress is strongest on the other.  Therefore a proof may
use the maximum of the two inequalities, or equivalently prove positivity of
their sum.

Exploratory evaluation over the already established cardinal-helper angle
range indicates that the symmetric sum has its minimum at the candidate for
moderate diagonal deviation.  This is numerical evidence only; the intended
next step is an analytic convexity/Taylor proof.

## Why this should be much faster

For a fixed graph, evaluating (4) requires only:

* five square angles;
* eight separator normals;
* six two-dimensional force vectors;
* the piecewise scalar function (3).

There are no (a_i,b_i,C_x,C_y) variables and no interval center propagation.

More importantly, on a region with fixed signs and fixed branches of (3),
the expression consists only of elementary sine/cosine terms and Euclidean
norms.  It is suitable for:

1. a direct hand monotonicity/convexity proof;
2. a Taylor-model proof on a handful of large boxes; or
3. tangent-half-angle substitution followed by an exact Bernstein/SOS
   polynomial positivity certificate.

Any of these is preferable to a 17-dimensional branch-and-bound tree.

## Exact candidate inner circle

There is a second equality structure worth exploiting.

Let

[
r_0^2=d^2+(d-h)^2
     =2d^2-2hd+	frac12.
	ag{6}
]

Because (t+d=	frac12+h), the two diagonal contacts are

[
P_{DW}=(-d,h-d)=(-d,t-	frac12),
]
[
P_{DS}=(h-d,-d)=(t-	frac12,-d),
]

and both lie on (|p|=r_0).

At the candidate, the six square intersections with the circle
(|p|=r_0) partition that circle exactly.  In cyclic order, the seams are

[
C/E,quad C/N,quad N/W,quad W/D,quad D/S,quad S/E.
]

The first four aligned seams are shared edge segments; the last two are the
two points above.  Numerically (r_0approx0.790913506).

This inner-circle equality is not yet a global lower bound, but it gives a
second possible hand-proof interpretation of the same self-stress: the
candidate is simultaneously tight in the outer containment support and in
an inner angular tiling.

## Current hand-proof target

The most promising analytic statement is now:

> For the candidate separator graph and the globally allowed cardinal-helper
> angle range, the sum of the stress (4) and its diagonal reflection is
> nonnegative at (R=R_*), with equality only at the candidate angles.

The existing four-side small-angle theorem already covers a neighborhood of
the equality point, so this statement only needs a strict estimate away from
that neighborhood.

Analogous stresses can be chosen for the three already identified alternate
(D)-axis graphs.  If the same monotonicity argument works there, the final
proof becomes a finite human case split by (D)-contact type rather than an
interval enumeration of center coordinates.
